The Twelve Steps

Step 1Acceptance

We admitted we were powerless over alcohol — that our lives had become unmanageable. Common themesBoundaries · Relief · Empowerment View reflections →

Step 2Hope

Came to believe that a Power greater than ourselves could restore us to sanity. Common themesFaith · Hope · Patience View reflections →

Step 3Faith

Made a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him. Common themesFreedom · Trust · Acceptance View reflections →

Step 4Courage

Made a searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves. Common themesHonesty · Courage · Growth View reflections →

Step 5Honesty

Admitted to God, to ourselves, and to another human being the exact nature of our wrongs. Common themesConnection · Honesty · Fear View reflections →

Step 6Patience

Were entirely ready to have God remove all these defects of character. Common themesIdentity · Surrender · Honesty View reflections →

Step 7Humility

Humbly asked Him to remove our shortcomings. Common themesPatience · Surrender · Trust View reflections →

Step 8Willingness

Made a list of all persons we had harmed, and became willing to make amends to them all. Common themesFreedom · Boundaries · Certainty View reflections →

Step 9Brotherly Love

Made direct amends to such people wherever possible, except when to do so would injure them or others. Common themesDetachment · Acceptance · Charity View reflections →

Step 10Integrity

Continued to take personal inventory and when we were wrong promptly admitted it. Common themesAcceptance · Accountability · Awareness View reflections →

Step 11Spiritual Awareness

Sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God as we understood Him, praying only for knowledge of His will for us and the power to carry that out. Common themesFaith · Meditation · Prayer View reflections →

Step 12Service

Having had a spiritual awakening as the result of these steps, we tried to carry this message to others, and to practice these principles in all our affairs. Common themesAwareness · Detachment · Integration View reflections →
