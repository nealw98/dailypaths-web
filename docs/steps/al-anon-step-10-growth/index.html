Step Ten: Keeping an Honest Inventory

Having completed the deep, transformative work of taking an inventory, sharing it, and making amends in Steps Four through Nine, we arrive at Step Ten. Step Ten states that we "Continued to take personal inventory and when we were wrong promptly admitted it".

Where the previous Steps helped us clean up the accumulated wreckage of our past, Step Ten introduces the practice of daily maintenance. It is the tool that keeps our spiritual house clean, preventing new clutter, unresolved resentments, and old reactive habits from piling up. Step Ten reminds us that recovery is not a static destination, but an ongoing way of living that requires daily self-awareness, honesty, and gentle self-correction.

The Two Modes of Inventory: Spot-Checks and Daily Reviews

In Al-Anon, we learn to practice Step Ten in two complementary ways: through moment-to-moment "spot-check" inventories and through quiet daily reviews.

  • The Spot-Check Inventory: Whenever we feel a sudden spike in anger, anxiety, resentment, or urge to control during the day, we pause and conduct a brief spot-check. We ask ourselves: "What is happening right now? Am I reacting out of fear, pride, or a desire to manage someone else?" This immediate check-in allows us to catch negative reactions in real time before they escalate into destructive arguments or emotional collapse.
  • The Daily Review: At the end of the day, we take a few quiet moments to reflect back over our choices, interactions, and attitudes. Just as in Step Four, a balanced daily review notices both our growth and our missteps. We give thanks for moments where we practiced patience, detachment, or self-care, while gently noting where we fell back into old habits.

Promptly Admitting Wrongs Without Defensiveness

The second half of Step Ten calls us to promptly admit when we are wrong. In the past, living with active alcoholism often made us defensive, rigid, or terrified of being wrong, as if admitting a mistake would expose us to criticism or defeat.

Step Ten reframes admitting a mistake from a sign of weakness into an act of spiritual strength and maturity. Admitting our errors "promptly"—as soon as we recognize them—prevents small misunderstandings from festering into deep resentments or guilt. We state our part simply, without making elaborate excuses, blaming others, or engaging in dramatic self-punishment. A prompt, simple admission clears the air, restores our integrity, and keeps our relationships grounded in mutual respect.

Keeping the Spotlight on Ourselves

A persistent challenge in practicing Step Ten is the subtle temptation to take other people's inventories instead of our own. When conflict arises, our old habits may urge us to analyze the alcoholic's flaws, monitor family members' choices, or justify our behavior based on what someone else did first.

Step Ten acts as a mirror that gently redirects the spotlight back to our own side of the street. It reminds us that while we cannot control or change another person's behavior, we are entirely accountable for our own responses, attitudes, and actions. By focusing strictly on what is ours to manage, we protect our inner peace and avoid falling back into the trap of self-righteousness.

Living Openly in Present-Moment Peace

Step Ten allows us to live in the present day without carrying the heavy baggage of yesterday or the fearful projections of tomorrow. By practicing daily self-examination and prompt admission, we cultivate a spirit of continuous willingness, humility, and growth.

Instead of hiding behind masks of perfection or living in fear of our flaws, we embrace our humanness with gentleness and humor. Step Ten keeps our connection to our Higher Power and our fellow human beings clear and open, ensuring that the serenity, dignity, and freedom we discovered in recovery remain a permanent part of our daily lives.
