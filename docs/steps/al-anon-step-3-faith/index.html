Step Three: Trusting Our Lives to a Higher Power

Having admitted in Step One that we are powerless over alcoholism and that our lives have become unmanageable, and having come to believe in Step Two that a Power greater than ourselves could restore us to sanity, we arrive naturally at Step Three. Step Three states that we "Made a decision to turn our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understood Him".

For many of us in Al-Anon, this Step marks a pivotal turning point in our recovery. Having discovered that our long-standing efforts to manage our own lives and control others were ineffective and exhausting, Step Three invites us to choose a different path—one grounded in faith, surrender, and spiritual partnership.

The Power of Making a Decision

The opening phrase of Step Three—"Made a decision"—reminds us that we have choices. After feeling trapped for years in automatic reactions to the illness of alcoholism, we discover that we possess the freedom to choose how we want to live.

Crucially, making a decision is a commitment to try rather than a demand for instant perfection. In Al-Anon, we often share a simple story to illustrate this distinction: three frogs sit on a lily pad, and one decides to jump off. How many frogs are left? The answer is three—because making the decision to jump is not the same as having jumped yet.

Step Three does not require us to immediately and flawlessly surrender every area of our lives. Instead, it asks for our willingness to begin stepping out of the way, trusting that we can learn to let go one day, one situation, or one person at a time.

Understanding "Our Will and Our Lives"

To turn over "our will and our lives," we must first understand what these words mean in the context of our daily struggles. In Al-Anon, our will represents our thinking, our desires, our expectations, and our persistent urge to control people and circumstances. Our life encompasses our daily actions, our relationships, our worries, and our future.

Before recovery, many of us operated out of deep-seated self-sufficiency, acting as the mother, nurse, caretaker, or manager of everyone around us. We believed that if we didn't figure everything out, anticipate every crisis, or manipulate events, everything would collapse.

Turning over our will and our lives means recognizing that this rigid self-reliance has failed us repeatedly. It means releasing the illusion that we know what is best for everyone else or that we can force a specific outcome. By making this decision, we stop setting ourselves up for failure and step down from trying to manage the universe.

One Person and One Problem at a Time

For most of us, the alcoholic in our lives is the very first person we need to turn over to a Higher Power. Having accepted in Step One that we cannot cure, control, or change their drinking, Step Three asks us to release our obsession with their choices. We hand over our urge to reform them, allowing them the dignity to face their own choices and consequences.

When facing major decisions or overwhelming crises, we often find it helpful to use concrete, practical tools to practice turning things over:

  • The "God Box": Many members write down a specific worry, fear, or uncontrollable situation on a slip of paper and place it inside a physical box. This physical gesture symbolizes transferring the burden from our minds into the care of our Higher Power.
  • Simple, Direct Prayers: Rather than using frantic prayers to dictate how God should fix a problem or cure the drinker, we use simple requests for guidance. Phrases such as "Please help me," "Keep me from dwelling on yesterdays and tomorrows," or "Let Go and Let God" help align our spirit with peace.
  • Pausing and Releasing Outcomes: When tempted to intervene or offer unasked-for advice, we remind ourselves that we do not have to know the future in advance. We do our footwork, state our personal limits, and leave the ultimate results in God's hands.

Freedom, Relief, and a New Foundation

Turning our will and our lives over to the care of God as we understand Him does not cause all of our external problems to vanish overnight. However, it fundamental changes our internal relationship to those problems.

The primary spiritual principle of Step Three is faith. When we stop trying to run the show, an immense weight is lifted from our shoulders. We no longer have to carry the suffocating guilt of feeling responsible for another adult's happiness or sobriety.

Together, Steps One, Two, and Three form the essential acceptance foundation of the Al-Anon program:

  • Step One provides honesty—acknowledging reality and our powerlessness.
  • Step Two provides hope—believing that help and sanity are possible.
  • Step Three provides faith—making the decision to trust a loving Power greater than ourselves.

With this foundation firmly in place, we are no longer lost or fighting alone. We opened the door to spiritual guidance and self-discovery, ready to move forward into the active healing Steps that follow.
