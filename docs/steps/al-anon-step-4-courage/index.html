Step Four: Taking an Honest Look at Ourselves

Having established a solid spiritual foundation in the first three acceptance Steps—admitting our powerlessness, opening our minds to hope, and deciding to trust a Power greater than ourselves—we arrive at Step Four. Step Four challenges us to take direct action through nine simple words: "Made a searching and fearless moral inventory of ourselves".

For many of us in Al-Anon, the prospect of taking a moral inventory can initially evoke deep anxiety and hesitation. Growing up or living alongside the disease of alcoholism often damaged our self-esteem, leaving us accustomed to harsh criticism and fear of self-examination. We may worry that looking inward will only expose an overwhelming list of flaws. Yet, Al-Anon literature reassures us that Step Four is not a tool for self-punishment or guilt; rather, it is an empowering journey of spiritual self-discovery that leads to genuine freedom and self-respect.

Approaching Inventory with Love, Kindness, and Balance

A central premise of Step Four in Al-Anon is that an inventory must be conducted with self-love, honesty, and strict balance. Without the acceptance and trust cultivated in Steps One, Two, and Three, an inventory can quickly become a weapon of self-abuse. The purpose of this Step is not to diminish an already damaged self-image, but to observe ourselves as we truly are—free from harsh judgment or self-hatred.

Crucially, a thorough inventory must include our positive character assets alongside our shortcomings. Because the effects of alcoholism often lead us to negate our worth and focus strictly on our faults, we frequently overlook the fact that we are terrifically caring, resilient, and loving people at heart. Locating and celebrating our special talents, unspoken strengths, and capacity for joy gives us the solid footing needed to examine our less desirable behaviors with objectivity.

Taking Stock: The Storekeeper's Perspective

Al-Anon literature offers a helpful analogy to demystify the process of taking inventory: that of a storekeeper evaluating stock. If we owned a toy store, we would periodically take inventory to see what items we have in excess and what items are in short supply—perhaps recognizing that we have too many dolls or not enough board games. Discovering these facts allows us to make necessary adjustments to bring our business into balance.

A personal inventory performs the exact same function. It allows us to take stock of our habits, assumptions, and relationships without berating ourselves for what is missing. It makes no sense to punish ourselves for being short on patience any more than a storekeeper would berate themselves for being low on teddy bears. By looking plainly at what we carry in our emotional inventory, we gain the clarity needed to keep what serves us, release what holds us back, and cultivate the qualities we wish to develop.

Uncovering Our Part and Moving Beyond Victimhood

Living in an alcoholic environment often encouraged us to adopt the role of a victim, blaming all of our misery, anger, and difficulties squarely on the drinker's behavior. While we did not cause the alcoholism, persisting in blame keeps us trapped in a state of helplessness. Step Four empowers us to step out of victimhood by examining our own motives and actions with rigorous honesty.

As we examine past situations and relationships, we begin to see our own part in them—where our attempts to control, manipulate, or please others contributed to our unmanageability. We often discover that many of our shortcomings were simply good traits taken to extreme lengths or old defense mechanisms that once protected us but no longer serve us today. Recognizing how our own perceptions and choices generated unnecessary misery allows us to take responsibility for our lives and realize that we have the power to choose differently going forward.

The Support of Sponsorship and Program Tools

Step Four is a thorough process, but it is one we never have to undertake alone. Al-Anon offers a variety of practical tools to guide us, including Conference Approved Literature such as the Blueprint for Progress workbook.

Working closely with a trusted sponsor or program friend is invaluable during this Step. A sponsor helps us maintain perspective when fear or self-criticism arises, reminding us to be gentle with ourselves and helping us recognize the fine qualities we might otherwise overlook. By sharing the journey with others who understand, we learn to trust the process, release our hidden secrets, and open our lives to the healing light of recovery.

Blueprint for Progress: 4th Step Inventory is Al-Anon’s workbook for taking an in-depth personal inventory.
