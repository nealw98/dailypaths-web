The Twelve Steps of Al-Anon

Key takeaways and core principles for each Step, with links to explore each Step and its daily reflections.

The Twelve Steps are the heart of the Al-Anon program. You do not have to take them in order, or all at once. Each card below summarizes one Step; follow its link to read more, or go straight to the reflections gathered under it.

Step One: Powerlessness & Unmanageability

Core principle Honesty & Surrender

  • The Illusion of Control: Attempting to manage, monitor, or curb another person's drinking leads to emotional exhaustion, hyper-vigilance, and personal unmanageability.
  • Enabling vs. Helping: Shielding an alcoholic from the natural consequences of their choices softens the impact of the disease, allowing drinking to continue while family members carry the burden.
  • The Three Cs: True recovery begins by accepting that you didn't cause the alcoholism, can't control its progression, and can't cure the illness.

Step Two: Restoration to Sanity

Core principle Hope & Open-Mindedness

  • Defining Sanity: Sanity in Al-Anon means seeing reality clearly rather than acting out of denial, panic, or the compulsion to repeat failing control tactics.
  • A Gentle Process: 'Came to believe' is written in the past tense, reassuring us that spiritual trust is a gradual unfolding rather than an instant requirement.
  • Individual Higher Power: Al-Anon is spiritual, not religious; a Higher Power can be God, nature, universal love, or the collective wisdom of the fellowship.

Step Three: Turning Will and Lives Over

Core principle Faith & Releasing Control

  • The Decision to Try: Making a decision is a commitment to step out of the way, not a demand for instant spiritual perfection.
  • Releasing Self-Will: Turning over our will means letting go of the urge to manage others, anticipate every crisis, or dictate how outcomes should look.
  • Practical Tools: Utilizing simple requests for guidance, the Serenity Prayer, and the 'God Box' helps transfer suffocating worries out of our minds.

Step Four: Searching and Fearless Inventory

Core principle Courage & Self-Examination

  • Balanced Self-Assessment: An inventory must evaluate both character shortcomings and positive assets with kindness and self-love.
  • The Storekeeper Analogy: Taking stock means observing habits and perceptions objectively to keep what serves recovery and release what causes unmanageability.
  • Moving Beyond Victimhood: Examining our part in past conflicts empowers us to take responsibility for our own choices and choices going forward.

Read about Step Four →Step Four reflections →

Step Five: Admitting Exact Nature of Wrongs

Core principle Vulnerability & Integrity

  • Threefold Order of Admission: We share our inventory with God, with ourselves, and with another trusted human being to break spiritual isolation.
  • Looking Beneath the Surface: Examining the 'exact nature' of our wrongs reveals the underlying fears, assumptions, and misguided survival motives driving them.
  • Finding Universal Connection: Sharing secrets with a sponsor or confidant replaces the fear of rejection with deep mutual understanding and relief.

Read about Step Five →Step Five reflections →

Step Six: Entirely Ready for Character Removal

Core principle Willingness & Acceptance

  • Defects as Former Armor: Shortcomings often originated as necessary survival mechanisms in chaotic environments that outlived their usefulness.
  • Willingness Over Self-Will: Readiness is a gradual process of letting go; forcing self-improvement through sheer willpower is just another attempt at control.
  • Revealing Underlying Assets: When character defects are cleared away by a Higher Power, the positive assets beneath (e.g. care replacing control) shine through.

Read about Step Six →Step Six reflections →

Step Seven: Humbly Asking Removal of Shortcomings

Core principle Humility & Active Trust

  • True Humility Defined: Humility is not groveling or humiliation, but seeing ourselves honestly in equal relationship to God and others.
  • Calm Asking: We ask for our shortcomings to be removed simply and directly, without bargaining, demanding, or treating our desires as all-important.
  • Practicing Alternatives: We actively cooperate with recovery by catching old habits in real time and choosing positive program slogans and responses.

Step Eight: List of Harmed Persons & Willingness

Core principle Responsibility & Compassion

  • Sorting Out True Harm: We separate actual personal responsibility from exaggerated, false guilt, focusing strictly on our own part in conflicts.
  • Self at the Top of the List: We acknowledge the harm inflicted upon ourselves through neglect, harsh self-criticism, and violated boundaries.
  • Gradual Willingness: Willingness is built in stages; we can begin by praying for the 'willingness to be willing' toward difficult relationships.

Step Nine: Making Direct Amends

Core principle Restitution & Personal Integrity

  • Amends vs. Apologies: True amends focus on taking responsibility, making restitution, and demonstrating altered behavior rather than seeking verbal pardon.
  • Exercising Discretion: Direct contact must be avoided if doing so would reopen wounds or cause further harm or distress to others.
  • Amends to Self: Forgiving our past mistakes, enforcing healthy boundaries, and practicing proper self-care restores our personal dignity and worth.

Read about Step Nine →Step Nine reflections →

Step Ten: Continuous Personal Inventory

Core principle Daily Self-Awareness & Maintenance

  • Two Modes of Review: Practicing real-time 'spot-checks' during emotional spikes and quiet end-of-day reflections keeps spiritual clutter from piling up.
  • Prompt Admission: Promptly admitting errors when we are wrong clears misunderstandings immediately and prevents resentments from festering.
  • Keeping the Spotlight on Ourselves: We avoid taking other people's inventories, focusing strictly on managing our own responses and attitudes.

Read about Step Ten →Step Ten reflections →

Step Eleven: Prayer and Meditation

Core principle Spiritual Connection & Guidance

  • Complementary Communication: Prayer is expressing our hearts to God; meditation is creating the quiet inner space to listen for guidance.
  • Praying for God's Will: We shift from giving God instructions to asking only for knowledge of His will and the power to carry it out.
  • Daily Anchor of Peace: Regular spiritual practice grounds our well-being in an unshakeable source of inner security and perspective.

Read about Step Eleven →Step Eleven reflections →

Step Twelve: Spiritual Awakening & Service

Core principle Gratitude & Living the Principles

  • Spiritual Awakening: A subtle metamorphosis in perception that restores our self-worth and allows us to see life through clarity and compassion.
  • Attraction Rather Than Promotion: We carry the message to others by modeling emotional stability, detachment with love, and genuine serenity.
  • Principles in All Our Affairs: Recovery becomes an active code for living, applying honesty, humility, and faith to every area of daily life.
