# Letting Go storyboard

## Editorial job

Move the reader from:

> If I stop managing this, I am failing someone I love.

to:

> I can care deeply, do what belongs to me, and place the outcome in the care
> of a Higher Power.

## Reader's emotional starting point

The reader is physically exhausted but mentally on duty. They scan the future
for the next crisis, prosecute the past with “if only” and “I should,” and
believe vigilance may still produce safety. Their own life has become smaller
around the work of managing someone else's.

## Emotional movement

Standing watch → bargaining for control → accepting an impossible assignment
→ recognizing fear beneath control → separating caring from carrying →
returning to what is mine → placing what is not mine in God's care → living
through uncertainty → allowing rest.

## Page sequence and component jobs

1. **The night shift** — visceral recognition; no advice yet.
2. **Pull quote:** “My body is in bed, but some part of me is still standing
   watch.”
3. **The promise hidden inside the worry** — expose the bargain that enough
   thought can prevent disaster.
4. **The Worry Loop insert** — recognition of the repeating mental pattern.
5. **The impossible assignment** — show the daytime behaviors and their cost.
6. **The painful recognition** — control and self-blame as responses to fear
   and powerlessness.
7. **Caring without carrying** — correct the fear that letting go is
   abandonment.
8. **What returns to me** — reclaim the choices, boundaries, truth, support,
   and life that remain available.
9. **What Returns to Me insert** — the responsibility distinction.
10. **Let Go and Let God insert** — answer the spiritual question: if I put
    this down, who holds it?
11. **Pull quote inside the spiritual insert:** “Faith does not always make
    letting go easy. It makes letting go possible.”
12. **Living through the discomfort** — letting go as a repeated practice,
    not a permanent feeling.
13. **Tonight's next honest action** — an immediate bedtime practice.
14. **Tonight's Next Honest Action insert** — practical close.
15. Final line: “The problem may still be here in the morning. I do not have
    to solve it in my mind all night.”
16. Four supporting Daily Paths readings.

## Topic boundaries and internal links

Letting Go is the inner release of responsibility for another person's
outcome. Neighboring topics should be linked only when they naturally enter
the story:

- **Higher Power and Trust** — where I place what I release. This is the
  primary cross-topic handoff and belongs in the spiritual insert.
- **Detachment with Love** — how I remain in relationship without absorbing
  another person's behavior.
- **Helping or Enabling?** — how release changes a specific offer of help.
- **Boundaries and Saying No** — what action may still belong to me.

Use descriptive topic-name links, not generic “Read more” links by themselves.
Resolve each canonical route from the current project before linking; do not
create duplicate topic records merely to satisfy the proposed slugs.
