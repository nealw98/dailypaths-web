# Finding Help Through Al-Anon

A place to begin when someone else's drinking is affecting your life.

## You do not have to wait for another crisis

Perhaps you check the time each evening, wondering when someone will come home and what state they will be in. Perhaps you make excuses for missed plans, keep family worries to yourself, or spend conversations talking about the drinker while struggling to explain how you are doing.

The effects may remain even after drinking stops or a relationship ends. You may still feel watchful, ashamed, or unsure of your own judgment. You might also wonder whether the problem is serious enough to ask for help. You can take the effect on your life seriously without first finding the perfect explanation for someone else's behavior.

This page introduces Al-Anon and gives you a few direct ways to make contact. You do not have to decide the future of your relationship before taking one of them.

## What Al-Anon offers

[Al-Anon Family Groups](https://al-anon.org/newcomers/what-is-al-anon-and-alateen/) brings together people affected by someone else's drinking. Members share what they have experienced and how they are learning to live differently. The focus is the affected person's life, whether or not the person who drinks seeks help.

That can be a shift when you arrive wanting a solution for someone else. You may begin by describing their latest argument or broken promise. Over time, there is room to speak about your fear, loneliness, anger, and the decisions you face. Hearing how other people have handled similar situations can give you something to consider without requiring you to copy their choices.

Meetings, the Twelve Steps, Al-Anon literature, and contact with other members are ways to participate. The program is spiritual and welcomes people of any faith or none. Al-Anon is peer support; it does not replace medical care, therapy, legal advice, or emergency help. You can use professional support alongside a meeting.

## Is it relevant to your situation?

Al-Anon's [official self-assessments](https://al-anon.org/newcomers/self-quiz/) include questions for adults affected by another person's drinking, adults who grew up with it, and teens. They are intended to help you consider whether Al-Anon or Alateen could help, rather than diagnose or label anyone.

Read the questions at your own pace. You might recognize the effort to hide what is happening, a preoccupation with another person's choices, or the sense that you are alone with the problem. You do not need to present your answers to Daily Paths. If something feels familiar, a meeting can be a way to explore it further.

:::insert One useful starting point

Read the [official adult self-assessment](https://al-anon.org/newcomers/self-quiz/adult-quiz/). Notice one question you would like to understand better. You can carry that question into a meeting even if you choose only to listen.

:::

## What a first meeting can be like

You can attend without a referral, and you can choose to listen rather than speak. Members generally share their own experience. You do not need a prepared account of your family or a decision about joining before you arrive.

For the practical details—finding the room, joining online, voluntary contributions, and what to do afterward—read [Your First Al-Anon Meeting](/articles/your-first-al-anon-meeting/). If you feel unsure about walking in or signing on, contacting the group beforehand may make the first step more manageable.

## Find a meeting or speak with someone local

Choose the kind of contact you can realistically make. An in-person meeting may give you a place to go outside your usual routine; an online meeting may fit around work or caring responsibilities. Check the listing for its time zone, language, access details, and any group-specific instructions.

:::insert Three ways to make contact

**Meet in person.** Use the [official Al-Anon meeting finder](https://al-anon.org/al-anon-meetings/find-an-al-anon-meeting/) for local in-person and hybrid options.

**Join online or by phone.** Browse [global electronic meetings](https://meetings.al-anon.org/electronic-meeting-page/) and check how to connect.

**Ask someone nearby.** Use [Worldwide Al-Anon Contacts](https://al-anon.org/al-anon-meetings/worldwide-al-anon-contacts/) to find an Al-Anon Information Service or General Service Office. A local contact can help with meeting information and practical questions.

:::

If you are looking on behalf of a young person, consult the official [Alateen information](https://al-anon.org/newcomers/teen-corner-alateen/). Check local arrangements rather than assuming that every group has the same age range or access requirements.

## Take one step you can name

You might save the details of one meeting, check which entrance it uses, or send a question through the group's listed contact method. A small, specific plan can be easier to carry out than “I should get help sometime.” If the first meeting does not feel like a fit, another group may offer a different experience.

Between meetings, [Daily Reflections](/reflections/) can help you pause and consider what is happening in your own life. Take a thought that matters to you into the day, or write down something you want to ask about. Reading can support daily practice alongside contact with other people.
