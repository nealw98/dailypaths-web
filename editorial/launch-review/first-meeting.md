# Your First Al-Anon Meeting

What to check beforehand, what you may encounter, and how to give yourself room to listen.

You have found a meeting. Now there are smaller questions: will you know which door to use? What happens if you cry? Will someone ask you to explain why you are there? It can be hard to take a practical step when you are already tired of managing unfamiliar situations.

You do not need to turn your life into a clear story before you attend. This orientation offers a few details to check so you can give your attention to the meeting itself.

## Before you go or sign on

Start with the [official meeting finder](https://al-anon.org/al-anon-meetings/find-an-al-anon-meeting/) or [electronic meeting list](https://meetings.al-anon.org/electronic-meeting-page/). Read the individual listing carefully. For an in-person meeting, look for room and entrance instructions as well as the street address. For an online meeting, check the time zone, joining link, and any password or contact instructions.

If you are unsure about access, the listed group contact or a [local Al-Anon Information Service](https://al-anon.org/al-anon-meetings/worldwide-al-anon-contacts/) may be able to answer your question. You could ask something as simple as, “I've never been before. Is this meeting suitable for a newcomer, and where do I go?” Ask about accessibility or language needs before travelling when possible.

:::insert A little less to remember

**Save the details:** the time, location or joining link, and any entrance instructions.

**Leave a little room:** avoid scheduling something immediately afterward if you can.

**For an online meeting:** choose somewhere you can listen privately and use headphones if helpful. Ask the group about its camera and participation practices.

:::

## You can begin by listening

Al-Anon's [newcomer FAQ](https://al-anon.org/newcomers/faq/) explains that speaking is your choice. A simple “I'm here to listen today” is enough if you are invited to share and do not want to. You can ask a question before or after the meeting if that feels easier than speaking to the group.

Formats vary. You may hear readings, a topic, or members talking about experiences in their own lives. Some things may feel immediately familiar; other language may take time to understand. You can notice what is useful without deciding how you feel about everything you hear.

If you do speak, you do not have to cover the whole history. You could name what brought you that day or something you are finding difficult. There is no need to make your experience sound more serious—or less serious—than it is.

## Cost, privacy, and unfamiliar language

There are no membership dues or fees. A group may invite voluntary contributions toward its expenses; this is different from an admission charge. Al-Anon treats anonymity and the confidentiality of meetings as basic principles. Respect other participants' privacy, and ask the group if you are unsure about an online setting or practice.

You may hear words such as “Higher Power,” “Steps,” or “sponsor.” You are allowed to ask what they mean. You do not have to find your own answer to every spiritual question during the first hour. The program's [official introduction](https://al-anon.org/newcomers/what-is-al-anon-and-alateen/) is another place to begin understanding its approach.

## After the meeting

Give yourself a moment before judging the whole experience. You may feel relieved, unsettled, tired, or unsure. It may help to write down one thing you recognized and one question you still have. If someone offers to answer questions afterward, you can choose whether to stay and talk.

Consider attending again or trying another group. You are looking for support you can use, not an experience you have to describe as perfect. A different time, format, or group may fit you better.

For a broader introduction and ways to make contact, return to [Finding Help Through Al-Anon](/guides/finding-help/). You can also read a [Daily Reflection](/reflections/) between meetings. You do not need to solve everything before taking another small step toward support.
