## A — In the Story Room, not on the site

### About the Al-Anon Program

How Al-Anon helps people affected by someone else’s drinking through meetings, the Twelve Steps, spirituality, sponsorship, fellowship, and daily practice.

By Daily Paths

Many people arrive at Al-Anon hoping to learn how to stop someone else’s drinking. They've tried talking, pleading, checking, covering for the person, or staying out of the way. Their days now revolve around what the other person will do next. It can be surprising to hear that Al-Anon offers a program for ther recovery. It gives them a place to look at what has happened to their own lives and find help with the choices still in their hands.

If you are wondering whether to attend, Finding Help: Could Al-Anon Be for You? can help you decide where to begin.

**What is Al-Anon?**

Al-Anon is a fellowship of people affected by someone else’s drinking. Members share their experience and support one another in a spiritual program of recovery. A member’s progress does not depend on the person who drinks getting sober. It isn't about that. People come to Al-Anon because of the effect that drinking has had on their life.

Al-Anon and Alcoholics Anonymous are separate fellowships. AA is for people seeking recovery from alcoholism; Al-Anon is for the families and friends affected by it. Al-Anon’s newcomer information explains whom the fellowship serves.

**Why would I need Al-Anon?**

Perhaps you listen for the door at night, check someone’s mood before making plans, or take on responsibilities that were never yours. You may have become so practiced at anticipating trouble that a quiet day still feels uncertain. These habits make sense in the circumstances that formed them, but they can leave very little room for your own life.

Recovery begins when you see that pattern without blaming yourself for the drinking. Ask yourself: What am I doing because I’m afraid of what might happen? What have I stopped doing for myself? Where do I say yes and then feel resentful? The answers show where a different choice is possible.

> You can begin your own recovery without waiting for someone else to change.

The Surrender guide explores what happens when you stop spending your strength on an outcome you cannot control.

**Meetings: learning from one another**

At a meeting, members share their experience, strength, and hope. You might hear someone describe a situation that is different from yours and yet still recognize the same fear, anger, or exhaustion. Someone may describe a choice you hadn’t considered: pausing before responding, asking for help, or allowing another person to handle the consequences of their own decision.

Listening is enough; no one is required to speak at a meeting. Over time, you find that sharing your own experience may help you notice what the drinking has cost you and how you respond when you’re frightened. The conversation gradually moves from what the other person did to what you need and what you can do today.

Anonymity helps make that honesty possible. Members are asked to protect the identity and personal sharing of others. Al-Anon’s FAQ explains more about meetings and participation. If you want to know what walking into a meeting may be like, read Your First Al-Anon Meeting: What to Expect.

**The Twelve Steps: a way to change**

The Twelve Steps are the program of recovery. Working the Steps with a sponsor brings a shift in perspective from the alcoholic to yourself and new choices and responses to people and situations. The Steps begin with an acceptance that we can't control another person and trying it has made our lives unmanageable. They invite members to seek help from a Higher Power, take an honest inventory of their own behavior, become willing to change, and make amends where they have caused harm. Later Steps bring that honesty into daily life through continuing inventory, prayer and meditation, and service to others.

Members return to the Steps as they grow. Read Al-Anon’s official wording of the Twelve Steps and explore their meaning with a sponsor.

**A spiritual program you can explore**

Al-Anon welcomes people with different beliefs, including people who are unsure what they believe. It is not a religious program. The program might refer to God or a Higher Power which may turn some people away from trying it. But you don't have to believe in what others believe. For someone who has spent years trying to solve every problem alone, even the willingness to ask for help can be the beginning.

Spiritual practice can start simply, by admitting “I don’t know what to do,” ask for guidance, and allow time to listen before you act. Honesty lets you name what is happening. Humility makes it possible to seek help. Acceptance lets you begin with the situation as it is today. None of these requires you to approve of harmful behavior or pretend you are comfortable with uncertainty.

Other members’ experiences can help you explore what a Higher Power, prayer, and meditation mean in your own practice. Al-Anon addresses spirituality in its newcomer FAQ.

**Sponsorship: someone to talk with between meetings**

A sponsor is an Al-Anon member who shares their experience of working the program with another member. It’s a place to ask questions about the Steps or talk through a situation that’s difficult to raise in a group. A sponsor may recognize a familiar fear when you are deciding whether to make another call, offer money, or have a hard conversation.

A sponsor shares experience; you remain responsible for your decisions. Listen at meetings for someone whose way of practicing the program speaks to you, then ask whether they’re available and what contact works for both of you. Sponsorship is voluntary. Al-Anon’s sponsorship information describes the relationship in more detail.

**The Wisdom of the Fellowship**

The fellowship carries wisdom that no one member could discover alone. Someone who has faced a familiar problem may share what helped them respond differently. Another member’s experience might stay with you until the day you need it. That’s how principles heard at a meeting become something you can use.

> The fellowship passes along what its members have learned by living it.

No one member has every answer. Over time, you collect words and experiences that help you act differently in your own circumstances. When you’re facing a hard decision between meetings, you have people to turn to as well as principles to remember.

**Steps, Traditions, and Concepts**

These three parts of Al-Anon have different purposes. The Steps guide personal recovery. The Traditions help groups stay united and focused on their purpose. The Concepts of Service guide how responsibility is shared throughout the fellowship. There’s no need to learn all three at once. With participation, they become less like names to memorize and more like principles you see in action. Read the Traditions and Concepts in Al-Anon’s official wording.

**Practicing the program each day**

What you hear in meetings becomes more useful when you carry it into the hours between them. Doing this reinforces the princiiple of One Day at a Time. But there is no single right schedule. A few moments throughout an ordinary day can give you a chance to remember a principle, ask for help, and notice how you are living it.

> A hard moment in the afternoon is where a principle learned in a meeting might get its first real test.

In the morning: Read the daily reflection or a passage from Al-Anon literature. Say a prayer for guidance, choose a principle to carry with you, and name a few things you are grateful for. The worries may still be there, but they need not be the only thing you bring into the day.

When something happens: Pause for a spot-check inventory: What am I feeling? What’s my part? A slogan, a prayer, or a call to your sponsor may help you choose your response. Jot down what you noticed and return to it later.

In the evening: Take an honest inventory. What disturbed my serenity today? Do I owe anyone an amend? What did I do well today? What am I grateful for tonight? The whole day was more than its hardest moment.

Download The Daily Paths app to keep tools for that routine on your phone: daily reflections and prayers for the morning; a gratitude list; a journal for a spot-check inventory, a question, or a thought you want to bring to your sponsor; and space to write a fuller evening inventory. Return to saved readings and listen to speakers when you want to hear another member’s experience. The tools help you bring the program into the day and give you something to take back to the fellowship.

**Keep coming back**

Understanding a principle at a meeting doesn’t mean you’ll remember it the next afternoon. Fear can bring back an old habit before you notice what’s happening. Bring that experience to your sponsor or your next meeting. There’s something to learn from it, and another chance to practice.

### The Al-Anon Program

A path for those affected by someone else’s drinking.

**What is Al-Anon?**

Al-Anon Family Groups are a fellowship of relatives and friends of alcoholics who share their experience, strength, and hope in order to solve their common problems. Al-Anon is not allied with any sect, denomination, political entity, organization, or institution.

The program is based on the Twelve Steps and Twelve Traditions, adapted from Alcoholics Anonymous. Members discover that they are not alone, and that they can find contentment and even happiness — whether the alcoholic is still drinking or not.

**Is Al-Anon for me?**

If a relative, friend, or coworker’s drinking is affecting your life, Al-Anon may be able to help. You don’t need to wait for the alcoholic to seek help before you begin your own recovery.

- Do you worry about how much someone else drinks?
- Do you tell lies to cover up for someone else’s drinking?
- Do you feel that if the drinker cared about you, they would stop?
- Do you feel angry, confused, or depressed most of the time?
- Do you feel there is no one who understands your problems?
If you answered yes to any of these, you are not alone. Al-Anon can help.

Take the full self-quiz at al-anon.org

Find a meeting

**Free, confidential, and open to anyone.**

Meetings are held in person and online around the world. There are no dues or fees for membership — you will never be asked to pay, and your attendance is completely anonymous.

In crisis?

Help is available 24/7.Call or text 988 in the USA.

**How the program works**

Al-Anon has no therapists and no mandatory course of treatment. Instead the program rests on four interconnected pillars — each one essential, none sufficient on its own.

The architecture

**The Twelve Steps**

A spiritual framework for personal growth. The Steps help members honestly examine their own attitudes and behaviors — not to fix the alcoholic, but to find freedom for themselves.

The community

**The Fellowship**

Regular gatherings — in person and online — where members share experience, strength, and hope. The fellowship is where isolation ends and recovery becomes real.

The wisdom

**The Literature**

Conference-Approved books, pamphlets, and daily readers developed by Al-Anon members for Al-Anon members — the written wisdom of families who walked this path before you.

The practice

**Service**

Giving back to the fellowship that gave so much to you. Service — from making coffee to sponsoring newcomers — is how members keep what they have by giving it away.

**Common questions**

**Is Al-Anon a religious program?**

No. Al-Anon is a spiritual program, not a religious one. Members are encouraged to find a Higher Power of their own understanding. There is no doctrine, no creed, and no requirement to believe anything in particular.

**Is my privacy protected?**

Yes. Anonymity is a foundational principle. What you share in a meeting stays in the meeting. Members use first names only, and no one will contact you unless you ask.

**What about young people?**

Alateen is part of the Al-Anon family, designed for younger members (ages 13–18) affected by someone else’s drinking.

More FAQs at al-anon.org

**Related fellowships**

Al-Anon is part of a wider family of twelve-step programs. These may also be helpful on your journey.
